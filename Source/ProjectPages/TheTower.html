The Tower is a videogame demo developed independently as a final project in university, with conception, planning, and development taking around eight months in total. The demo is approximately two hours in playtime, with rudimentary assets, mechanics, and content, with the focus of realising the core idea behind the ambitious project within the short timeframe.

The aim of the project was to realise and sustain a sensation of sublime in a 3D action roleplaying game. That is to say, the sense of awe and wonder felt by players of excellent roleplaying games. The efficacy of both The Elder Scrolls IV: Oblivion (Bethesda Game Studios, 2006) and Dark Souls (FromSoftware, 2011) in this realm has been analysed by Martin (2011) and Vella (2015) respectively; and the foundation for this project draws upon that literature whilst critiquing and developing upon it.

The approach taken adopts Vella's observation of obfuscation of a game's precise working supporting sublime's longevity, while recognising that if such is done too liberally it has the opposite effect.

The game's world, story, unique mechanics, and so forth, were all carefully considered with the purpose of achieving the sublime. These were implemented in rudimentary form, tested independently, then tested again in carefully designed user tests, to evaluate efficacy and inform the next development sprint within an agile methodology.

A pervasive philosophy of the game's design was how every aspect should be considered and adapted or removed according tot he extent tow hich it supports the game's design. An example of a philosophy conducive to this, in respect to achieving the sublime, is that all features of the game should exist to expand the player's conception of possibilities, rather than limiting them.
