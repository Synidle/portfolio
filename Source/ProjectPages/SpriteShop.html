This web application was developed in a period of about 2 months for an interactive web-application development module at university. My application received a grade of 86, remarked as having an engaging and rather addictive user experience.

N.B. I do not maintain this project so it does not work as smoothly nor reliably as it did at submission.

Systems Design

This application takes the formula of typical "cookie-clicker" sites intended as basic distractions, and introduces engagement beyond the arbitrary increasing of numbers:

  • Profit can be augmented through a fun minigame
  • Profit can be creatively spent towards customising a character

That rewards fund customisation tangibly rewards the player, in a manner often missing from minigames.

The application utilises a poly-hierarchy to facilitate a multifaceted system involving a loop of investment, feedback, and reward that can be interrupted by a minigame.

Game Design

A core feature for design was a game that could be played to increase monetary balance at the risk of losses. I had the following requirements:

  • Played by two (player & computer)
  • Balance of skill and luck
  • Easy to pick up
  • Fun and addictive gameplay
  • Unpredictable: the game can be turned around at any time

I wished this to be a card game, to suit the "casino" context, inspired by the minigames from Super Mario 64 DS (Nintendo EAD, 2004). However, no existing card games comfortably fit my requirements, so I created my own: Court Jester.

I was inspired by the fun and straightforward gameplay of Uno (Merle Robbins, 1971) and the unpredictability of Exploding Kittens (Lee, E. & Small, S., 2015) — both of which work best with sizeable groups of people, so requiring adaptation towards my primary requirement.

User Experience Design

The project exhibits strong UX design, with focus on:

  • Accessibility
  • Ease of use
  • Enjoyment

This was achieved through:

  • Thorough use of affordances
  • Clarity of current state
  • Clear objectives
  • Multiple paths to objective
  • Helpful organisation of information

Pages are kept minimalist while directing the user where to click for further information or content.

Soft, "friendly" colours are used throughout to fit the relaxed, leisurely theme of the project.

Bolder colours and animations are used sparingly and for the benefit of making the card game more engaging and moreover directing the player where to click, reducing their mental load.

Court Jester was intentionally implemented using HTML-elements only, for the benefit of accessibility: it can even be played with a screen-reader.

The application adapts to screen-width, enabling play on multiple devices, or to be open on the side while simultaneously doing something else. On extremely narrow displays, superfluous content is removed, or content is abbreviated, or put into the background.

Future Opportunity

I would develop the sprite feature towards more a virtual-pet system, with mechanics beyond customisation.

Customisation can also be enhanced by introducing additional animals that can be chosen beyond the default cat.

A fully-realised product would also involve inter-player features to encourage customisation and expression, with minigames also playable between players. This might work over LAN, to create a fun digital passtime for people within the same environment, such as a school computer suite.
