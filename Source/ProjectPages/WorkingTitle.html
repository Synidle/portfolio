Concept

The problem I wanted to solve was effectively combining the emotional quality of great horror games with engaging core gameplay and combat.

Survival and psychological horror games can be effectively scary but the gameplay is often intentionally simplistic or repetitive. On the other hand, "action" horror games tend to forego the fear and helplessness felt in true horror games in favour of fast-paced combat.

I decided to try a fusion of survival and psychological horror with dungeon-crawler, because I could tap into features of dungeon-crawlers complementary to horror:

  • Journeys into the unknown.
  • Threatening encounters.
  • Secrets.

The theme of the unknown, facilitated by dungeon crawlers, pairs particularly well with psychological horror, so I decided to lean into this.

To strike a balance betwen the engaging gameplay of dungeon crawlers and the sense of vulnerability in survival horror, beyond standard enemies I decided to add a persistent threat that could not be overcome through normal means.

Features

The design was informed by an extensive and thorough research endeavour concerning relevant games. The greatest consequence of this was a list of what to avoid with my project.

I also conducted interviews with potential players and released forms regarding horror game and dungeon crawler features, to better understand the problem space.

Then I produced lists of essential and desirable features, some of which are as follows:

  • Dungeon
    • Exploration focus.
    • Contains various secrets, puzzles, interactables.
    • Rooms only appear after entering them, creating uncertainty.
  • Psychological horror
    • Disturbing atmosphere to contribute to a sustained sense of dread.
  • Real-time combat
    • Weaker enemies to make the dungeon more engaging.
    • Items that allow the player to grow stronger.
  • Main antagonist
    • Focus of game.
    • Vastly stronger than the player; cannot be defeated through simple means.
  • Story
    • Environmental storytelling.
    • Fragmented storytelling.
    • Leaves things unexplained.
  • Eery/uncanny aesthetic

Dungeon

Inspired by the Persona (Atlus, 1996) series, I decided to make the setting a school, as not only would it enable engaging NPCs and side-stories as in Persona, but also would allow me to introduce the horror element gradually — suitable for a foreboding horror concerning secrets and the unknown.

Enemies

The theme of enemies is the corruption of ordinary elements of the environment.

For example, the mimic enemy (common in dungeon crawlers) turns the innocuous chest (usually one of the dungeon objectives, and a source of relief/excitement) into a dangerous enemy encounter. I made my mimic particularly grotesque and frightening to shock the player, and also made it relinquish a key item on death so as to make the encounter mandatory. The mimic is, however, hidden in a branching path, initially suggesting to the player that they have found a secret, and so to put down their guard.

Conclusion

My prototype implementation proved that an engaging and immersive fusion of dungeon crawler and psychological horror would be possible following this design.
