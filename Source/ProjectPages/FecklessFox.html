Feckless Fox was created for a university assessment requiring the creation of an interactive 3D environment in the Unity game engine. My solution was to create a 3D platforming level with a custom character controller, with a complex level design of multiple paths and areas, multiple collectables, and an upgrade item enabling further exploration on replaying the level. I received a grade of 91 for this project: "an exceptional experience through and through... like a demo of a professional game akin to the inspiration example mentioned in the report", with "complexity and fun coming from the rich level design" and being "easy pick up and play for all audience types".

The project took a period of around three months to complete.

Feckless Fox is inspired by 3D platformers of the '90s and early '00s, such as Mario 64 (Nintendo, 1996), Sonic Adventure (Sonic Team, 1998), and Rayman 2 (Ubi Pictures, 1999), aspiring to achieve the same timelessness achieved by an aesthetic appealing to children and adults alike, and accommodation of all skill levels.

Level Design

My level's design had the following objectives:

  • Teach a newly-unlocked mechanic.
  • Teach the player the level's mechanics using the environment.
  • Appropriate for an early (2nd or 3rd) level.
  • Appropriate for different skill levels.
  • Rising challenge.
  • Platforming on organic elements.
  • Build an atmosphere.

The level shown is early in the game, introducing the spin-jump mechanic. The level design teaches this mechanic. It is designed with replayability in mind, with multiple routes of varying challenge, secrets to find, and routes accessible only on replaying the level with the spin-jump unlocked.

Additionally, I wanted to convey a kind of narrative through level design alone, without dialogue; and moreover have the player have particular feelings during different parts of the level, and depending on how the level is approached.

The setting is a town overtaken by rats, falling into disrepair. The environment reflects this.

The level begins on the streets of the town, with options for exploration, but also a clear forwards route. Most danger is posed by enemies, not platforming. The route is enclosed, and there is some foreshadowing of the later sewer section. Gradually the player moves higher, with the platforming becoming more hazardous. Just as the player believes themselves to be returning to the safety of the ground of the town, they fall into the sewer. Here the spin-jump is introduced, first in a safe environment, then in progressively tricky platforming segments. Ambient noise, green glow, and labyrinthine structure make this area feel treacherous; its confined nature contrasts with the earlier section, invoking a sense of danger. After a challenging platforming sequence the level ends, ideally leading to another sewer level. Replaying the level with the spin-jump allows access to new routes and an alternative end. Launching across rooftops offers a new perspective to the level, and empowers the player.

The level, taking a lot of inspiration from Bloodborne's (FromSoftware, 2015) Central Yharnam, has a dark atmosphere. This juxtaposition with the child-friendly 3D platformer genre of the game makes it more interesting and increases the scope of what can be done by the game, and also broadens the target audience. The platformers mentioned as the main inspirations similarly increase their scope by introducing unsettling environments or darker themes; Feckless Fox forefronts this more greatly.

The setting of the level is inspired by the authentic town environments of Sonic Unleashed (Sonic Team, 2008), which are fun to explore and make the world feel real and immersive.

The goal was to make organic use of environmental elements. For example, platforming inside and outside houses and around towers.

Positioning of items, points of interest, and objects to allow for directional light and shadow, indicate where to go. I considered what the player's view should be during all sections of the level, particularly the light from the moon and the height variation of buildings.

Implementation

Spot-lights are positioned to produce shadows for visual effect. Lighting colour, position, direction, intensity, all contribute to an aesthetic.

A flickering fire was made by having the light-source and fire image vary in intensity according to Perlin noise. The sound was that of a shower, reduced in pitch, through a low-pass filter.

3D sound effects are used to invoke feelings, such as trepidation through the wind sound when upon the walls, and increased proximituy to the source escalates the feeling. I made all sound effects by recording household items and editing the recording, or by producing a sound using a MIDI editor and filters.

I enhanced performance by using simplified meshes for the collision part of level geometry.

Particles emphasise effect and dynamics, particularly where there is a hazard. Here large particules rise and change from white to green and fade to transparent, imitating the effect of a waterfall.

Gameplay

While the game is best played with a controller, I ensured that it can also be played with a keyboard, to increase its accessibility.

Challenges Overcome

When having a friend test the level, he climbed up the fallen lamppost at the start � which I had not foreseen � so breaking out of bounds. I appreciated this creative use of organic decorative pieces as traversable level pieces, so rather than preventing walking up the lamppost, or completely sealing off the area it led to, I incorporated that area into the level as a secret, and hid an item there to reward attentive players.

The dark atmosphere created a challenge of how to illustrate the path. I had to make best use of everything at my disposal. In the screenshot below, the next platform is illuminated, and the shadow aligns with the succeeding platform. Contrasting materials also highlighted platforms.

There were some challenges faced in programming. For example, how to handle enemy behaviour within my state machine system, given enemies are motivate by AI rather than player input. I considered moving action code � such as executing movement � into State classes so that state and behaviour become entwined, but doing such would mean my classes were no longer limited to exact purposes. Instead, I used my existing code but with two state machines for enemies: the action state machine is the same as that of the player � what the enemy is doing; the behaviour state machine is for AI specifically � what the enemy is intending. Enemies simultaneously have an action state and a behaviour state.

Another programming issue was how to handle interaction with moving platforms, given the simple and traditional method of reparenting the player to the platform on contact worked inconsistently, and changing the player object's position in the scene heirarchy is undesirable and has the potential to cause future problems. I therefore implemented a system whereby I can assign the player a "virtual parent", namely a variable used in position calculations to achieve the effect of reparenting the player without needing to do so.

Reflection

I believe that the experience is aesthetically strong, the level design is captivating, and the gameplay is intuitive and enjoyable. Particularly the first town section feels very authentic and ambient, and I enjoy the distinct areas and challenges within.

The greatest problem for me is the lack of background detail. After the first town section, the level feels less like a real town, and more like platforms floating in space. I would like to populare background areas with terrain, buildings, and interesting features.

The town is also lacking interesting details; only the same assets are used. There should be more signs of life, particularly inside the buildings. Also, there should be more visible evidence of the ruin brought by the rats. More diegetic platforms, such as scaffolding or debris, might replace existing ones.

I would reorganise the level to strengthen the feeling of being within a city. The set-pieces should be more closely packed, creating an enclosed level rather than a stretched one. Beneficially, this enables additional creative use of the spin-jump.

I would like to enhance the game with a flying enemy with a projectile attack, adding variety to platforming via a mobile obstacle.

Moreover, shaders might be used to add texture to the liquids.

Finally, I would consider adding music, becaues I do not like the moments of silence; but the music must be appropriate and not detract from the ambience. It must therefore be tailor-made, not added for the sake of having music.
